1. Project Summary

In commercial applications—such as e-commerce promotions, ad-tech conversion bidding, and healthcare treatments—outcomes \(Y \ge 0\) exhibit severe zero inflation (90–99% exact zeros) and heavy right tails. Causal ML practitioners routinely apply concave transformations, universally \(g(y) = \log(1 + ay)\) or \(\text{asinh}(ay)\), to stabilize variance and rein in outlier leverage. While Chen & Roth (2024) recently proved that the average treatment effect (ATE) of log-like transformations depends arbitrarily on unit scaling \(a\) (e.g. dollars vs. cents), its consequences for individual-level uplift rankings and targeting policies had remained unstudied.

This project investigates whether the choice of reporting scale \(a\) changes who gets targeted, quantifies commercial policy regret, and evaluates whether count-aware native likelihood models restore scale equivariance without ad-hoc transformations. Across controlled Monte Carlo simulations, oracle benchmark grids, and a randomized commercial field trial (\(N=64{,}000\)), we show that: (1) varying \(a\) under \(\log(1+ay)\) replaces up to 48.2% of top-20% targeted customers on real marketing spend; (2) as \(a \to 0\), rankings target Level Dollars (\(\tau_{\text{level}}\)), but as \(a \to \infty\), rankings converge strictly to Conversion Probability (\(\tau_{\text{extensive}}\)), completely ignoring spend magnitude; and (3) Compound Poisson-Gamma (Tweedie, \(p=1.5\)) and Two-Part Hurdle estimators achieve exact \(\rho = 1.0000\) scale equivariance and generate the highest empirical policy value ($0.8566 vs $0.7302 per user).

3. Datasets Evaluated

  • Synthetic & Oracle Zero-Heavy Benchmarks (\(N=20{,}000\), 6 Scenarios, 100 Replications): Configurable two-margin DGPs with known ground-truth CATE across zero proportions \(p_0 \in \{0.70, 0.90, 0.98\}\).
  • Hillstrom MineThatData Randomized Marketing Field Trial (\(N=64{,}000\)): Genuine randomized retail marketing trial (Men's \(N=42{,}613\), Women's \(N=42{,}693\), Control \(N=21{,}307\)) with 99.1% zero spend. Evaluated across continuous budget fractions \(k \in [0.01, 0.50]\) and scales \(a \in [10^{-3}, 10^3]\).
  • Criteo Uplift v2.1 Semi-Synthetic Benchmark (\(N=200{,}000\)): Real 12-dimensional advertising features paired with controlled two-margin synthetic spend.

4. Novelty & Research Contributions

  • Extension of Chen & Roth (2024) to CATE & Uplift: Proves mathematically and empirically that concave transformations silently alter the targeted estimand, converting monetary revenue targeting into binary conversion targeting as scale increases.
  • Count-Aware Likelihoods for Uplift: First benchmark applying compound Poisson-Gamma (Tweedie deviance, \(p=1.5\)) and Hurdle models inside cross-fitted meta-learners for zero-heavy continuous spend.
  • Separation of Estimand Warping from Estimation Noise: Oracle ranking analysis proves ranking collapse is an inherent mathematical pathology of concave transformations, not an estimation artifact.
  • Practical Decision Framework: Provides an actionable decision flowchart for practitioners detailing when \(\log(1+ay)\) is acceptable and when Tweedie is mandatory.

5. Key Empirical Learnings & Results

  • H1 (Ranking Instability) — Validated: Under \(\log(1+ay)\), Spearman rank stability drops to \(\rho = 0.5660\) and Top-20% overlap drops to \(35.87\%\) when extensive and intensive margins diverge.
  • H2 (Transformation Growth Rate) — Validated: Strictly power transforms (\(y^{1/2}\)) and rank percentiles preserve scale equivariance (\(\rho \equiv 1.000\)), whereas slow-growth logarithmic transforms suffer severe rank inversion.
  • H3 (Count Model Equivariance) — Validated: Tweedie and Hurdle maintain exact scale-equivariance (\(\rho = 1.0000\), \(100\%\) overlap) across all unit scalings \(a \in [10^{-3}, 10^3]\) and zero rates (\(70\% - 98\%\)).
  • H4 (Policy Welfare Maximization) — Validated: On the Hillstrom field trial, Tweedie attains the peak empirical IPW policy value of $0.8566 / user at \(k=0.20\), outperforming naive level models ($0.7302) and log-transformed regression ($0.7831) by +$0.12 / targeted user.
